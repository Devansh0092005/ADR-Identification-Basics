### ADR Identification Basics

For each patient case, use this framework:

1. **Identify the symptoms**

   * What new or worsening symptoms occurred?
   * When did they begin?

2. **Review medication history**

   * List all medications, including dose and start date if provided.
   * Note any recently started, stopped, or dose-adjusted drugs.

3. **Establish the timeline**

   * Did the symptoms begin after the suspected medication was started or its dose changed?
   * Did they improve after stopping it (**dechallenge**)?
   * Did they return after restarting it (**rechallenge**), if applicable?

4. **Assess whether the drug is a plausible cause**

   * Is the reaction consistent with a known adverse effect?
   * Are there alternative explanations, such as another disease or medication?

5. **Identify the suspected drug**

   * Name the medication most plausibly associated with the reaction.
   * If more than one drug could be responsible, identify the alternatives and explain why.

6. **Document the ADR**

   * **Suspected drug:** …
   * **Reaction:** …
   * **Onset/timeline:** …
   * **Evidence supporting drug relationship:** …
   * **Alternative causes:** …
   * **Conclusion:** Probable / Possible / Unlikely, with justification.
# ADR Identification Basics

## Patient Case

A 55-year-old patient was diagnosed with hypertension and was started on **amlodipine 5 mg once daily**. After approximately two weeks of treatment, the patient developed **swelling of both ankles and feet**. The patient had no history of significant leg swelling before starting the medication. After consultation with the doctor, amlodipine was discontinued and an alternative antihypertensive was prescribed. The ankle swelling gradually improved.

## ADR Identification

**Suspected drug:** Amlodipine

**Suspected adverse drug reaction:** Peripheral edema (ankle/foot swelling)

**Time relationship:** The swelling developed approximately two weeks after starting amlodipine.

**Evidence supporting the relationship:**

* The symptom appeared after initiation of the medication.
* The patient did not have the symptom before treatment.
* Peripheral edema is a recognized adverse effect of amlodipine.
* The swelling improved after the suspected drug was discontinued (positive dechallenge).

**Alternative causes:** Other causes of peripheral edema should be considered, including cardiac, renal, hepatic, or venous conditions. However, the timing and improvement after discontinuation support amlodipine as the suspected cause.

## Conclusion

The patient's **ankle and foot swelling is a suspected adverse drug reaction to amlodipine**, with the temporal relationship and improvement after withdrawal supporting a drug-related cause.
